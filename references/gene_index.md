# Curated Gene Index

Genes and CNVs commonly returned in autism / neurodevelopmental testing where published
care guidance exists.

## How to use this file

Each entry gives you: the syndrome, the **authoritative sources to fetch**, the **care
domains** those sources cover, and any **gene-specific trap**.

**It deliberately does not contain screening ages, intervals, or drug doses.** Those
drift between guideline versions and must be read from the current source with a
retrieval date. See `risk_layer_policy.md`.

**Tier** refers to the risk-layer tier of the *surveillance content* for that gene.

**If a gene is not here**, search GeneReviews → ClinGen → OMIM → recent literature, and
report honestly how much established guidance exists. "No published surveillance
protocol" is a valid and useful finding.

**Universal sources** worth checking for any gene:
[GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1116/) ·
[ClinGen](https://clinicalgenome.org/) ·
[ClinVar](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/) ·
[OMIM](https://www.omim.org/) ·
[SFARI Gene](https://gene.sfari.org/) ·
[Simons Searchlight](https://www.simonssearchlight.org/) (registry, enrols by gene) ·
[ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/)

## Tumour-predisposition genes — Tier 1, highest priority

These carry cancer surveillance protocols beginning in childhood. **Getting these in
front of a family is the highest-value output of this entire skill.**

### PTEN — PTEN Hamartoma Tumor Syndrome (PHTS)
- **Presents as:** autism/NDD with macrocephaly is a recognised presentation
- **Sources:** [International PHTS Consensus Guidelines, *Clin Cancer Res* 2025](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/31/9/1754/761247/Cancer-and-Overgrowth-Manifestations-of-PTEN) · [Pediatric surveillance update, *Clin Cancer Res* 2025](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/31/2/234/751094/Update-on-Pediatric-Surveillance-Recommendations) · [German pediatric guideline, *Mol Cell Pediatr* 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40348-022-00135-1) · [GeneReviews NBK1488](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1488/)
- **Domains:** thyroid, breast, renal, endometrial, colorectal, skin; overgrowth
- **Trap:** macrocephaly + NDD is often the *only* presenting feature in childhood, so
  the cancer risk is easily overlooked. Surveillance begins in childhood.

### TSC1 / TSC2 — Tuberous Sclerosis Complex
- **Sources:** [Pediatric surveillance update, *Clin Cancer Res* 2025](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/31/2/234/751094/Update-on-Pediatric-Surveillance-Recommendations) · [GeneReviews NBK1220](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1220/) · [Tuberous Sclerosis Alliance](https://www.tsalliance.org/)
- **Domains:** renal angiomyolipoma, SEGA, cardiac rhabdomyoma, pulmonary LAM, epilepsy,
  skin, ophthalmic, neuropsychiatric (TAND)
- **Trap:** mTOR inhibitors are an established targeted therapy — one of the few in this
  space. Worth surfacing that targeted treatment exists.

### NF1 — Neurofibromatosis type 1
- **Sources:** [GeneReviews NBK1109](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1109/) · [Children's Tumor Foundation](https://www.ctf.org/)
- **Domains:** optic pathway glioma, plexiform neurofibroma, MPNST, blood pressure,
  skeletal, learning/attention
- **Trap:** learning and attention difficulties are core features, not incidental.

### DICER1 — DICER1 tumour predisposition
- **Sources:** [Pediatric surveillance update, *Clin Cancer Res* 2025](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/31/2/234/751094/Update-on-Pediatric-Surveillance-Recommendations) · [GeneReviews NBK196157](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK196157/)
- **Domains:** pleuropulmonary blastoma, thyroid, ovarian
- **Trap:** more often a secondary finding than a primary NDD result — see the secondary
  findings section of `risk_layer_policy.md`.

## Recurrent CNVs — Tier 1

### 22q11.2 deletion syndrome
- **Sources:** [AAP Health Supervision clinical report, *Pediatrics* 2025;156(2)](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/156/2/e2025072717/202658/Health-Supervision-for-Children-With-22q11-2) · [Adult management recommendations, *Genetics in Medicine* 2023](https://www.gimjournal.org/article/S1098-3600(22)01028-0/fulltext) · [22q11.2 Society guidelines](https://22qsociety.org/knowledge/guidelines/) · [22q.org](https://22q.org/)
- **Domains (Tier 1):** cardiac, immune/thymic, calcium/parathyroid, palate/speech,
  hearing, renal, skeletal
- **Domains (Tier 2):** elevated psychiatric risk in adolescence and adulthood, with
  monitoring value — frame per Tier 2 rules
- **Trap:** hypocalcaemia can present acutely and is easily missed. Immune status affects
  live-vaccine decisions. Both are practical, immediate, and often unknown to families.

### 16p11.2 deletion / duplication
- **Sources:** [GeneReviews NBK11167](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11167/) · [Simons Searchlight](https://www.simonssearchlight.org/)
- **Domains:** weight trajectory (deletion → obesity risk; duplication → underweight),
  epilepsy, spine, speech/language
- **Trap:** deletion and duplication have partly opposite phenotypes. Confirm which.

### 15q11-q13 duplication (dup15q) / UBE3A (Angelman)
- **Sources:** [GeneReviews — Angelman NBK1144](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1144/) · [Dup15q Alliance](https://dup15q.org/) · [Angelman Syndrome Foundation](https://www.angelman.org/)
- **Domains:** epilepsy (often severe), sleep, feeding, scoliosis, SUDEP counselling
  where indicated
- **Trap:** active therapeutic pipeline — trials are worth surfacing.

## Ion channel and synaptic genes — treatment-relevant

### SCN2A
- **Sources:** [*Brain* 2024;147(8):2761](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/147/8/2761/7656659) · [*Trends in Molecular Medicine* 2025](https://www.cell.com/trends/molecular-medicine/abstract/S1471-4914(25)00216-3) · [FamilieSCN2A Foundation](https://www.scn2a.org/) · [GeneReviews NBK594499](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK594499/)
- **Domains:** epilepsy management, developmental support, movement
- **Trap — important:** **direction of effect changes the drug.** Gain-of-function
  variants, typically neonatal-onset seizures, showed 94% good-to-excellent phenytoin
  response; loss-of-function variants, typically autism/ID with later or no seizures,
  showed 0% (*Brain*, 2024). **You cannot infer direction from the variant notation
  alone** — it requires functional data. Say that the distinction exists and is
  clinically decisive, and that the genetics/neurology team should establish it. Do not
  guess a direction, and never imply a drug choice.
- **Also:** individualised antisense oligonucleotide therapy has reached patients
  (*Nature Medicine*, 2026), and the strategy depended on functional class. Worth
  surfacing as an active area, not as an available treatment.

### SCN8A, SCN1A
- **Sources:** GeneReviews; relevant epilepsy foundations
- **Trap:** same direction-of-effect problem. For SCN1A specifically, sodium channel
  blockers are generally avoided in Dravet — this is well established and clinically
  urgent, so make sure the family's neurology team is engaged.

### GRIN2A / GRIN2B
- **Sources:** GeneReviews; [CureGRIN Foundation](https://curegrin.org/)
- **Trap:** gain- versus loss-of-function again determines management approach.

### CACNA1C — includes Timothy syndrome
- **Sources:** GeneReviews
- **Trap — urgent:** cardiac conduction involvement. Any suspicion warrants prompt
  cardiology input. Do not soften this.

### STXBP1
- **Sources:** GeneReviews; [STXBP1 Foundation](https://www.stxbp1disorders.org/)
- **Domains:** epilepsy, movement disorder, developmental support

## Chromatin, synaptic scaffold, and transcriptional regulators

### MECP2 — Rett syndrome
- **Sources:** [GeneReviews NBK1497](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1497/) · [International Rett Syndrome Foundation](https://www.rettsyndrome.org/)
- **Domains (Tier 1):** cardiac QT monitoring, scoliosis, breathing dysrhythmia, bone
  density, nutrition/GI, epilepsy
- **Trap:** QT prolongation has medication implications. An approved targeted therapy
  exists — check current status rather than asserting from memory.

### SHANK3 / 22q13 deletion — Phelan-McDermid syndrome
- **Sources:** [GeneReviews NBK1198](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1198/) · [Phelan-McDermid Syndrome Foundation](https://pmsf.org/)
- **Domains (Tier 1):** renal, cardiac, hearing/vision, epilepsy, lymphoedema
- **Domains (Tier 2):** adolescent/adult regression and catatonia are recognised
  features. Families who know this seek help much earlier — this is a strong example of
  Tier 2 content preventing harm.

### SYNGAP1
- **Sources:** GeneReviews; [SynGAP Research Fund](https://curesyngap1.org/) · [SFARI ASO programme](https://www.sfari.org/funded-project/development-of-antisense-oligonucleotides-for-syngap1-haploinsufficiency-associated-with-autism-spectrum-disorder-and-intellectual-disability/)
- **Domains:** epilepsy (often myoclonic/absence), sleep, GI, pain insensitivity
- **Trap:** strong patient organisation and active therapeutic pipeline — high value to
  surface. Registry enrolment is meaningful for these families.

### CHD8
- **Sources:** GeneReviews; SFARI Gene
- **Domains:** macrocephaly, GI (constipation is prominent and under-treated), sleep

### ADNP — Helsmoortel-Van der Aa syndrome
- **Sources:** GeneReviews; [ADNP Kids](https://adnpkids.com/)
- **Domains:** vision, early tooth eruption (a recognised diagnostic clue), GI, feeding

### ARID1B — Coffin-Siris; ANKRD11 — KBG syndrome
- **Sources:** GeneReviews for each
- **Domains:** growth, feeding, hearing, dental, skeletal, epilepsy

### DYRK1A
- **Sources:** GeneReviews; [DYRK1A Syndrome International Association](https://dyrk1a.org/)
- **Domains:** microcephaly, feeding/growth, epilepsy, vision

### FMR1 — Fragile X syndrome
- **Sources:** [GeneReviews NBK1384](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1384/) · [National Fragile X Foundation](https://fragilex.org/)
- **Domains:** connective tissue, mitral valve, recurrent otitis, seizures, anxiety
- **Trap — family-wide:** premutation carriers face distinct adult-onset risks (FXTAS,
  FXPOI). This has cascade implications for relatives. **Route to genetic counselling
  rather than elaborating** — see the children and secondary-findings sections of
  `risk_layer_policy.md`.

### NRXN1
- **Sources:** GeneReviews; SFARI Gene
- **Trap:** deletions carry documented association with psychiatric conditions — Tier 2
  framing, and be careful not to alarm.

### PPP2R5D, SETD5, AUTS2, KMT2A, MED13L, POGZ
- **Sources:** GeneReviews where available; SFARI Gene; Simons Searchlight
- **Note:** most have emerging natural-history data rather than surveillance protocols.
  Registry enrolment is often the most useful concrete action to offer.

## Where to look for the non-medical asks

Families frequently want things this index doesn't cover — schools, benefits, therapies,
local support. Those are legitimate needs and often more pressing than surveillance.

- **Registries and natural history studies:** [Simons Searchlight](https://www.simonssearchlight.org/) enrols by gene and is the single best general pointer
- **Gene-specific organisations:** linked per gene above; these are usually the best
  source of practical, lived-experience guidance
- **Trials:** [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/) — search by gene and by
  syndrome name, since both are used

If asked for support that is outside your scope, say so and point to the gene-specific
organisation. They will do it better than you can.
